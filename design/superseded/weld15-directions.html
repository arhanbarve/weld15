Where it sits

The office gave you rooms and dimensions but no adjacency you could draw. The sentence that unlocks it is “immediately to your right will be the common room” paired with “turn to the left… and go down the hallway.” That makes the entry a T: common room one way, hallway the other. Chain those pieces end to end and they come to about 51 feet — which is almost exactly Weld's clear interior width. The suite spans the building.

ACROSS THE BUILDING (east–west) common room 15 + entry + hallway 23 + far bedroom 10 + walls 3 = 51 ft Weld Hall exterior width 54 ft − two 1.5 ft masonry walls = 51 ft clear fits THROUGH THE BAND (north–south) hallway 4 + bedroom depth 16 = 20 ft kitchen 10 x 12 bumps south behind the common room — the one room with no window WHAT DOESN'T CLOSE far bedroom reads 16 x 10, but its slot in the band is 16 x 16 ~96 sq ft unaccounted — closets, a chimney breast, or the north wall steps here

That last block is drawn as hatching, not invented rooms. The office warned you their numbers could be off by a foot in either direction, so the model treats every dimension as a parameter you can drag rather than a fact baked into geometry. In September you walk in with a tape measure and the plan corrects itself.

Decision two

Which way does the suite face?

Weld 15 is first floor, north end — that part is documented. What nobody has told you is whether the common room sits at the Old Yard end or the Widener end. It decides the view out your windows and which rooms get morning light, so it is worth getting right. If you don't know, pick the last option and it becomes a switch in the app.

What is “K”?

A 10 × 12 room with no exterior wall, marked K on a 1995 blueprint. Harvard's own dorm data says Weld's two common rooms each have a galley kitchen, so a kitchen in a first-floor suite is more plausible than the resident assumed. Model it as a kitchen, a study, or storage? It changes what gets built in there.

How many of you?

Two bedrooms at 160 sq ft each reads as four people in two doubles. Weld 15 held seven undergraduates in 1983 and the first-floor suites are the big ones. Bed count drives every furniture decision.

Is the JFK history in or out?

The Crimson traced Kennedy's freshman room — original Weld 32, first floor north — to what renumbering in 1962 turned into Weld 15. Your room. This can be a quiet plaque you find by clicking a window, a full 1936 layer you toggle against 2026, or left alone.
