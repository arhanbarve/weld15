# P1 — Geometry core, no rendering

Six pure modules. No three.js scene, no React, no camera. Everything here is testable in Node,
which is the point: this is the most consequential logic in the project and the cheapest place to
be wrong.

---

## Objective

`buildSuite(params)` and its supporting maths, with a test suite that would catch every failure
mode I can think of. Nothing renders in this phase.

**Done when:** `npm test` passes with the invariant suite below, and I have pasted the output
including the computed suite area and the analytic solar checks.

## Scrutiny before writing

### 1. Four coordinate frames, and they must never be confused

This is where a project like this rots. Naming them explicitly and converting only through
`frames.ts`:

| Frame | Axes | Origin | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WGS84** | lat, lon degrees | — | the source GIS data only |
| **Site** | x east, y north, feet | Weld's centroid | `campus.json`, `weld.json` as stored |
| **Building** | u across, v along, feet | Weld's centroid, rotated −13.2° | placing the suite in Weld |
| **Suite** | x across band, y back from gable | inside face of the north gable | `rooms.ts`, all room rects |
| **Three** | x east, **y up**, z south | Weld's centroid | the renderer, from P2 on |

The one that bites: three.js is Y-up, our plan data is Y-north. `toThree(x, y, z) => [x, z, -y]`.
Getting that wrong mirrors the whole building, and mirroring is exactly the ambiguity the project
already has, so a silent mirror would be invisible. **Tested as a round trip in both directions.**

### 2. Which quantities are free and which are consequences

Earlier drafts treated the bathroom depth and the hall width as independent, then quietly adjusted
them until the arithmetic closed. That hides the closure rather than showing it. Correct split:

**Free** (from the resident, or a real measurement later): `clearWidth`, `bandDepth`, `unknownW`, `bedBW`,
`bathW`, `bedAW`, `commonW`, `bedDepth`, `unknownD`, `kW`, `kD`, `partition`, `masonry`, `ceiling`.

**Derived** (consequences, never set directly): `bathDepth = bedDepth − partition − unknownD`,
`hallWidth = bandDepth − bedDepth − partition`, `hallLength`, every room's position, `area`.

**Residuals** (reported, not forced): `acrossResidual = clearWidth − (columns + partitions)`.
Defaults give 0. A slider is allowed to break closure, and the UI shows by how much rather than
silently absorbing it. That is more honest than a layout that always looks perfect.

### 3. The winding trap, already proven present

P0 measured 35 clockwise rings and 1 counter-clockwise. `extrude.ts` normalises to
counter-clockwise before emitting side quads, or that building gets inverted normals and renders
black. Not hypothetical — the data is in the repo and the test asserts both windings exist.

### 4. Correcting a claim I made earlier

I wrote that the suite "gets almost no direct sunlight" because it faces north. **That is wrong**,
and I checked it numerically rather than repeating it:

A wall is lit when the sun's azimuth is within 90° of the wall's normal. Weld's north gable faces
azimuth 13.2°, so it is lit for sun azimuths between −76.8° and 103.2°.

| Date | Sunrise azimuth | Gable lit in the morning? |
|---|---|---|
| Summer solstice | 57.4° | yes |
| Equinox | 90.0° | yes, barely |
| Winter solstice | 122.6° | no |

So the gable catches **low, raking direct sun early morning and late evening from roughly late
February through late October** — the crossover is at declination −9.7° — and none at all in
winter. That is a much better thing for the time-of-day slider to teach than a flat "no sun".

Analytic values, used as test fixtures because they need no external source:

```
solar noon altitude = 90 - latitude + declination
  summer solstice   71.07 deg
  equinox           47.63 deg
  winter solstice    24.19 deg
sunrise azimuth: cos(az) = sin(declination) / cos(latitude)
  summer 57.4   equinox 90.0   winter 122.6
```

### 5. Wall generation must conserve area

The strongest available correctness test for `walls.ts`: rooms plus unknown regions plus wall bands
must exactly tile the suite's footprint, with no overlaps and no gaps. If area is conserved to
within a rounding epsilon, the wall bands are correct. This is what catches the doubled-stroke class
of bug the SVG prototype had.

## Invariants the test suite asserts

**frames.ts**
- site → building → site round trips within 1e-9 ft
- site → three → site round trips
- a point due north in site frame lands on −Z in three frame
- rotating Weld's ring into the building frame gives a bounding box of 54 × 151 ± 0.5, which is the
  real proof the 13.2° figure is right

**rooms.ts**
- default params give `acrossResidual === 0`, `depthResidual === 0`, `stackResidual === 0`
- `bathDepth === 8.5`, `hallWidth === 3.5` as consequences, not inputs
- suite area 897.5 ± 0.5 sq ft; unknown regions 189 sq ft, excluded from that total
- no two room rects overlap — checked at defaults and over 500 randomised param sets
- every room reachable from the hall through a door, so nothing is landlocked
- mirroring twice is the identity
- all four placements keep the suite inside Weld's footprint polygon

**walls.ts**
- rooms + unknowns + walls tile the footprint, area conserved within 0.01 sq ft
- no two wall bands overlap
- every opening lies wholly within exactly one wall band
- exterior walls appear only on the band boundary; partitions never do
- the bathroom door is on a wall the bathroom actually touches — the bug from revision 2, asserted

**solar.ts**
- the three noon altitudes above, within 0.15°
- the three sunrise azimuths above, within 0.5°
- azimuth at solar noon is 180° ± 0.5
- gable is lit on 21 June at 06:00 and unlit on 21 December at 09:00 — the corrected claim, asserted
- altitude is negative at local midnight on every date sampled

**collide.ts**
- AABB overlap is symmetric, and touching-but-not-overlapping is not an overlap
- point-in-polygon on Weld's real 59-point ring: centroid inside, a point 200 ft away outside
- wall snap moves a piece to flush contact, never through the wall

**extrude.ts**
- `normalizeRing` makes every campus ring counter-clockwise, all 36
- signed volume equals footprint area × height within 0.5% — divergence theorem, catches flipped
  faces and missing caps at once
- every side-quad normal points away from the ring centroid
- vertex and index counts match the ring length, so no silent degenerate triangles
